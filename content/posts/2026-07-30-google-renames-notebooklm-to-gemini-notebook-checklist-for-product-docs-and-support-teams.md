- What happened: Google renamed NotebookLM to "Gemini Notebook." See the announcement: https://www.theverge.com/tech/966112/google-gemini-notebook-notebooklm.
- What to do now: update user-facing text, links, and key screenshots. Prioritize high-traffic pages first.

## Why this matters (for real teams)

- Search and discovery: users will search for "Gemini Notebook." If your help pages or FAQs still use the old name, organic traffic can drop. Monitor clicks and impressions for both names (https://www.theverge.com/tech/966112/google-gemini-notebook-notebooklm).
- Support volume: expect a short spike in tickets after a rename. Prepare canned replies and a one-line public note to reduce repeat questions.
- Contracts and integrations: record any formal references to "NotebookLM" in contracts, API docs, or privacy notices so legal can review. Even a single mention may require an audit.

Rule of thumb: treat this as a branding migration first — update copy, links, and discovery before changing deep integrations. Source: https://www.theverge.com/tech/966112/google-gemini-notebook-notebooklm.

## Concrete example: what this looks like in practice

Scenario: a two-person startup links to a NotebookLM setup guide in onboarding emails.

Steps to fix (ordered, with time estimates):
1. Update the email headline to: "Open notes in Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM)." Add Google’s announcement link: https://www.theverge.com/tech/966112/google-gemini-notebook-notebooklm. (5–10 minutes)
2. Replace or annotate screenshots. If you can’t re-export images, add an overlay caption: "Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM)." (15–60 minutes)
3. If you own the linked page, add a 301 redirect from the old path or update the URL target. Test the redirect in staging. (30–120 minutes; verify for 72 hours)
4. Create a one-line support template: "NotebookLM is now Gemini Notebook; functionality unchanged" plus the announcement link. Keep it under 160 characters. (10–30 minutes)
5. Track search queries for both "NotebookLM" and "Gemini Notebook." Set alerts for drops in clicks or rises in support tickets and review for 7–14 days.

Test: verify redirects for 72 hours and watch support/search signals for at least 7 days after changes (https://www.theverge.com/tech/966112/google-gemini-notebook-notebooklm).

## What small teams and solo founders should do now

If you are a solo founder or a team of ≤5, focus on high-impact, low-effort tasks you can finish in one sprint.

Top-priority checklist (solo / small-team actionable tasks):
- [ ] Run a sitewide search for the exact string "NotebookLM." Log each hit and mark pages with >100 monthly visits as high priority. Goal: initial pass in 2 business days. (Tool tip: grep, CMS search, or simple site: query.)
- [ ] Update key customer touchpoints: change onboarding emails, README, and top 3 help articles to read "Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM)." Aim to complete these 3 updates in <1 business day.
- [ ] Add or request a 301 redirect for any owned links that users click in emails. Verify the redirect in staging and check for 72 hours.
- [ ] Add a 160-character support reply and pin it in your help center. Example: "NotebookLM is now Gemini Notebook; functionality unchanged — see Google’s note: https://www.theverge.com/tech/966112/google-gemini-notebook-notebooklm." (5–15 minutes)
- [ ] Tag analytics: capture clicks on both name variants. Create a 14-day dashboard; if you have fewer than 1,000 pageviews, sample weekly instead of daily.

Additional operational tips for solo founders:
- Batch edits into a 90–120 minute session to reduce context switching.
- Prioritize pages with >3 conversions per week or >100 visits/month.
- If you charge $0–$50 for onboarding services, include an update in the next invoice email to reduce support calls.
