## TL;DR (jobs + people, plain English)

- What happened: The Verge reports that Microsoft agreed with the American Federation of Teachers (AFT) and the United Federation of Teachers (UFT) on a set of school-focused AI privacy commitments presented as ten principles. Reported highlights include a promise not to train models on student or educator data, narrower data collection, bans on some student-facing “AI companion” features, and plain-language family disclosures. Source: https://www.theverge.com/policy/992359/microsoft-aft-schools-ai-privacy

- Who this affects at work
  - Classroom staff: teachers, TAs, librarians — those who pick and supervise classroom tools.
  - School operations: IT directors, procurement officers, data-protection officers, contract managers.
  - District leaders and union negotiators: those who sign addenda and set policy.
  - Vendors and company teams: product managers, engineers, legal, sales, and founders who sell to schools.
  - Note: the reporting frames these as contract principles to be used by districts and unions as bargaining or procurement tools. Source: https://www.theverge.com/policy/992359/microsoft-aft-schools-ai-privacy

- Short practical actions by role
  - Teachers: pause broad classroom rollout until you have a family-facing disclosure and a written vendor attestation about training on student data.
  - IT / Procurement: require a signed vendor addendum and family FAQ before any student-facing pilot.
  - Founders / Product: default to collecting less student data, prepare an addendum aligned to the reported principles, and offer district-level terms. Source: https://www.theverge.com/policy/992359/microsoft-aft-schools-ai-privacy

- Quick example: If a vendor offers a homework chatbot, stop the pilot unless the vendor provides a written attestation that student uploads won’t be used to retrain models and supplies a family-facing FAQ. Source: https://www.theverge.com/policy/992359/microsoft-aft-schools-ai-privacy

## What the sources actually say

The Verge article summarizes an agreement between Microsoft and the AFT/UFT that lays out ten principles districts and unions can press vendors to accept. Reported commitments include: not training models on student or educator data; narrowing data collection and retention; banning certain student-facing “AI companion” features; and providing plain-language disclosures for families. Source: https://www.theverge.com/policy/992359/microsoft-aft-schools-ai-privacy

Methodology note: this memo interprets the reported principles for roles and operational steps; where a numeric or procedural detail is not in the reporting, it is listed later under Assumptions / Hypotheses. Source: https://www.theverge.com/policy/992359/microsoft-aft-schools-ai-privacy

## Which tasks are exposed vs which jobs change slowly

Exposed tasks (operational, actionable):
- Approving or enabling student-facing chatbots, tutors, or companion features flagged as risky in the report; require vendor attestation and family disclosure first. Source: https://www.theverge.com/policy/992359/microsoft-aft-schools-ai-privacy
- Uploading student essays, voice recordings, or homework to third-party model-training flows; stop or quarantine these uploads until contracts clarify use.
- Adding procurement gates: route student-facing features to legal, privacy, and union stakeholders before pilots.

Slower-change jobs and processes (policy, bargaining, product strategy):
- District-level procurement language, collective-bargaining clauses, and labor agreements — these typically evolve through negotiation and formal cycles.
- Core pedagogy and curriculum design — teachers and unions will adapt, but classroom practice is less likely to change overnight.

Use this practical triage: block or require attestations for student-facing inputs now; plan contract and policy changes through procurement and bargaining channels over the medium term. Source: https://www.theverge.com/policy/992359/microsoft-aft-schools-ai-privacy

## What employees should do now

Teachers and classroom staff
- Pause routine use of student-facing AI tools unless the vendor gives a written family disclosure and a clear statement about whether student inputs will be used for model training. Source: https://www.theverge.com/policy/992359/microsoft-aft-schools-ai-privacy
- Keep a one-page usage log for any pilot: who used the tool, the task, which students, and dates.
- Do not upload sensitive personal data to external services without legal clearance.

IT / Procurement teams
- Add a contract review step that flags student-facing features and routes items to legal and union reps.
- Require vendors to supply family-facing FAQs and answers about data flows; include basic audit rights.

Operational checklist for teachers and IT (short)
- [ ] Vendor provides a plain-language family disclosure (FAQ)
- [ ] Vendor confirms in writing whether student inputs are used for model training
- [ ] Pilot is limited to a named cohort and documented in a usage log
- [ ] Legal or procurement has signed an addendum before rollout

Source: https://www.theverge.com/policy/992359/microsoft-aft-schools-ai-privacy

## What founders and managers should do now

Product managers and engineers
- Prioritize data minimization for student-facing features and separate telemetry from model-training pipelines.
- Add a launch gate that requires legal sign-off and a family FAQ before classroom rollout.

Legal, sales, and GTM teams
- Draft a contract addendum aligned to the reported ten principles. Include clear language about training, collection limits, and family disclosures.
- Offer district-level terms and be prepared for procurement negotiation and union involvement. Source: https://www.theverge.com/policy/992359/microsoft-aft-schools-ai-privacy

Operations and support
- Log pilot details in CRM and ticketing systems. Track which schools signed addenda and which teachers issued parental notices.

## Checklist and next steps

### Assumptions / Hypotheses
- The Verge reporting accurately describes a package of ten principles and commitments, including a promise not to train models on student/educator data and clearer family disclosures. Source: https://www.theverge.com/policy/992359/microsoft-aft-schools-ai-privacy
- Operational thresholds below translate those principles into procurement and product rules; they are hypotheses to test in contract talks:
  - 0%: vendor attestation that student inputs are not used to train models (a contractual claim to seek).
  - 90%+: target reduction for optional telemetry fields in student-facing defaults (proposal to implement).
  - Pilot cohort: up to 30 students as a controlled test group for initial trials.
  - Pilot duration: up to 14 days for an initial field test unless a longer term is contractually approved.
  - Procurement negotiation window: anticipate 30–90 days to finalize addenda with large districts.

### Risks / Mitigations
- Risk: Vendor statements are ambiguous or unverifiable.
  - Mitigation: require written attestations, audit rights, and periodic attestations (for example, every 30 days during active pilots).
- Risk: Teachers or parents do not understand disclosures.
  - Mitigation: supply a one-page summary and require an in-class verbal briefing; translate FAQs into the primary family languages.
- Risk: Product defaults continue to collect excessive student data.
  - Mitigation: implement automatic retention limits (e.g., raw inputs <= 30 days) and opt-in gates for any training-related use.

### Next steps
- For district IT / procurement officers: adopt a 5-question procurement gate and require a signed vendor addendum before student-facing pilots. Key questions: Will the vendor train models on student data? Can the vendor provide a plain-language family disclosure? Will the vendor accept audit rights? Which exact data fields are collected? What is the retention period? Source: https://www.theverge.com/policy/992359/microsoft-aft-schools-ai-privacy
- For teachers: use the classroom checklist above, keep a one-page usage log for each pilot, and limit pilots unless an addendum is signed.
- For founders and product teams: produce a contract-addendum package and a family FAQ; change defaults to avoid using student data for training unless a district explicitly agrees.

Quick procurement checklist (actionable)
- [ ] Vendor provides clear family-facing disclosure
- [ ] Vendor states in writing whether student inputs are used for model training
- [ ] Audit rights or attestations are available for review
- [ ] Data-minimization and retention policy documented
- [ ] Contract addendum references the reported principles (ten principles) and is signed before pilot

Final note: The Verge piece offers a contract-oriented template that jobs across schools and vendors — teachers, IT staff, procurement officers, product managers, legal teams, and founders — should convert into concrete contract language, operational gates, and family-facing materials before any student-facing rollout.
